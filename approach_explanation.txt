Traffic Demand Prediction - Approach Explanation
=================================================

PROBLEM SUMMARY
---------------
Predict traffic demand (0-1 float) for given geohash locations at specific timestamps.
Evaluation metric: max(0, 100 * R2_score)

APPROACH
--------
We used LightGBM with 5-Fold Cross-Validation and extensive feature engineering.

OOF R2 Score: ~0.9955  →  Competition Score: ~99.55 / 100

FEATURE ENGINEERING
-------------------
1. Temporal Features:
   - Parsed timestamp (H:MM) into hour and minute
   - Time-of-day in minutes (0-1439)
   - Cyclical encoding using sin/cos for hour and time_of_day (captures periodicity)
   - Binary flags: is_morning_rush (7-9am), is_evening_rush (5-7pm), is_midday, is_night

2. Road Features:
   - RoadType ordinal encoding: Residential=1, Street=2, Highway=3 (missing=0)
   - LargeVehicles binary: Allowed=1, Not Allowed=0
   - Landmarks binary: Yes=1, No=0

3. Weather & Temperature:
   - Weather ordinal encoding: Sunny=3, Rainy=2, Foggy=1, Snowy=0
   - Temperature filled with mean for missing values
   - Temperature squared (non-linear temperature effects)

4. Target Encoding (Most Powerful Features):
   - Geohash statistics: mean, std, median, min, max of historical demand
   - Geohash × Timestamp interaction: mean & std (captures location-specific time patterns)
   - Timestamp global statistics: mean & std
   - Hour-level statistics: mean & std
   These features encode spatial and temporal demand patterns from training data.

MODEL
-----
- LightGBM Regressor with 5-Fold Cross-Validation
- Parameters:
  - n_estimators: 2000 (with early stopping, patience=100)
  - learning_rate: 0.03
  - num_leaves: 127
  - feature_fraction: 0.8, bagging_fraction: 0.8 (prevent overfitting)
  - L1 (alpha=0.1) and L2 (lambda=0.1) regularization

KEY INSIGHT
-----------
The geohash + timestamp interaction feature (geo_ts_mean) is the most powerful predictor.
It captures the historical demand at a specific location at a specific time, which makes
intuitive sense: rush hour demand at a highway junction is consistently high, while
residential areas are quiet at night.

Since test data is for day 49 but training has mostly day 48 data, the geohash-based
historical features effectively transfer location patterns across days.

TOOLS USED

- Python 3, pandas, numpy
- LightGBM for gradient boosting
- scikit-learn (KFold, r2_score)
